IM Resident rules

For hospitalist reference only

⚠️ For hospitalist attendings only

This section covers internal medicine resident workflow and caps to help hospitalists coordinate care. These are NOT hospitalist operations rules. Confirm with the team for real-time status.

🔄 Call cycle
The call cycle runs: Long → Post → Short → Pre, then repeats.

Long call

Full teach team: new patient cap 8, or up to 4 new patients per intern (3 during first 8 weeks of AY). Senior resident: up to 5 new patients. Overnight patient assignments count toward new patient cap if intern census reaches 5+. 1 new patient max after 5:30pm. No admits after 6pm. Sign out from post-call team no sooner than 4pm if criteria met.

Post call

Full teach team: finish out 4 new patients on long call per intern (3 in first 8 weeks). Thu-Sun: interns off. Sign out as early as 4pm to long call only if criteria met. When interns are off, resident handles all patient care and completes all notes.

Short call

Full teach team: new patient cap 4, or up to 2 new patients per intern. Senior resident: up to 3 new patients. 1 new patient max after 3:30pm. No admits after 4pm. 15-20 min attending-lead chalk talk.

Pre call

Full teach team: finish out 2 new patients on short call per intern. Thu-Sun: resident off. Didactics 3-4pm. Discharge planning before 6pm. Bedside rounding highly encouraged.

📊 Patient caps
Intern cap: 9 (8 during first 8 weeks of AY)
PGY-2 junior hospitalist cap: 10
PGY-3 junior hospitalist cap: 12
MS3 cap: 4 (notes cosigned by intern)
Sub-I cap: 6 (notes cosigned by resident)
Team cap: 18 (MS3/Sub-I presence does NOT change this)
📉 Low census rule
Residents can be assigned overnight admissions to bring census up to:
4 patients for interns  ·  5 for PGY-2  ·  6 for PGY-3
🚪 Sign out rules
Standard: 6:00 PM to night float. Must use I-PASS method.

Early sign out (4pm) criteria — ALL must be met:
• Patients stable, family updated, loops closed with consults
• ICU aware of sick patients
• No major pending tests
• Discharges prepared (pended orders + summaries)
• Printed list and verbal sign-out given to long call team

Post-call days: Can sign out at 4pm if criteria met.
All other days: Earliest 6pm.
🌙 Night float
Hours: 6:00 PM – 7:00 AM

Intern: up to 4 patients (3 in first 8 weeks)  ·  Resident: up to 3 (4 in first 8 weeks)
Max 2 admits after 4:00 AM  ·  1 after 4:30 AM  ·  No admits after 5:00 AM

Days off: Intern off Sunday  ·  Resident off Saturday (with covering senior resident)
🔁 Bouncebacks
Includes ICU downgrades and ED/UC admissions within a 7-day discharge period if a team member knows the patient. Continuity (by COLOR) required before transfer or discharge.

• Do NOT count as new admission but DO count toward census cap
• If present before 6pm (4pm on post-call), team completes the admission

🏥 Hospitalist role with teaching teams

Hospitalists are consultants on teaching team patients — strictly prohibited from entering orders or being the first call at any time unless during conference. All bedside evaluations and orders must be managed by house staff.

📚 Academics
Morning report / noon conference: Mon-Fri 11:15 AM - 1:00 PM
Chalk talks on short call days: 15-20 min, attending-led
Pre-call didactics: 3:00-4:00 PM, residents + attending
Discharge rounds: Daily — plan discharges, pend orders, prepare summaries. Discharge orders signed by 10 AM daily (10/12/2 initiative).

How to use this tool

Tips & feedback

🎯 What this tool does

This assistant is built from your group's official documents — co-management agreements, surge protocols, shift guidelines, and onboarding materials. It gives you fast, policy-grounded answers at the point of care. It is a reference tool, not a substitute for clinical judgment or direct communication with colleagues.

🗂 The five tools
Each tab is built for a specific task.
💬 Operations Q&A
Ask anything about shift operations in plain language. Best for: shift hours, patient caps, cross cover, jeopardy, ICU hold list, direct admissions, JADE team, Epic workflows, surge pay, and more.
Tip: tap a chip to try a common question first.
☀️ Daytime surge & 🌙 PM surge trial
Step-by-step calculators that walk you through the exact surge algorithm. Enter your numbers and get a yes/no with the math shown. Use these when deciding whether to call in surge — they remove the guesswork.
🗺 Patient routing
Two ways to determine site (Green vs Memorial) and service attribution (hospitalist primary, specialty primary, or consult):
• Guided flow — tap through specialty → scenario → verdict. Best for straightforward cases.
• Describe patient — type a free-text description and get an instant recommendation. Best for complex or atypical cases.
Tip: site selection always comes first — the tool will flag if a patient is at the wrong site.
📋 Onboarding
New hire checklist, phone numbers, door codes, meeting schedules, and division website. Share this with new group members on their first day.
💡 How to ask questions that get the best answers
The more context you give, the more precise the answer.
✓ Include the site
Good: "72M hip fracture at Green — who is primary?"
Vague: "hip fracture — who admits?"
✓ Include post-op day for surgical patients
Good: "POD 10 hip replacement with wound infection at Green"
Vague: "post-op hip patient with infection"
✓ Include the time for time-sensitive questions
Good: "acute cholecystitis at Green at 11pm — who admits?"
Vague: "cholecystitis at Green"
✓ Be specific about the clinical scenario
Good: "58M with lymphoma, neutropenic fever, no hematologic issue — Green or Memorial?"
Vague: "oncology patient with fever"
✓ For surge questions, give your numbers
Good: "6 hospitalists on service including Red Team, census 75 at Green — can I call surge?"
Vague: "can I call surge today?"
⚠️ Important limitations

Always use clinical judgment

• This tool reflects your group's policies as written in the source documents. Policies evolve — when in doubt, confirm with a colleague or division leadership. • Grey areas exist in every co-management agreement. The tool will flag them and suggest direct physician-to-physician communication. • For urgent clinical decisions, call the relevant attending directly — don't rely solely on this tool. • This tool does not have access to real-time census data, EMR, or patient information.
